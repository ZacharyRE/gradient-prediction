# Literature informing this study

These are design motivations, not evidence that the proposed LoRA surrogate works.

- [Jaderberg et al., Synthetic Gradients, ICML 2017](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/jaderberg17a.html): predict a downstream error signal to decouple an expensive suffix. Our existing predictor is already a gradient surrogate in this sense. We additionally require actual downstream quality and complete elapsed-time comparisons.
- [Czarnecki et al., Sobolev Training, NeurIPS 2017](https://arxiv.org/html/1706.04859v3): learn function values and derivatives together, including a loss-critic formulation for synthetic gradients. **New experiment:** fit a compact scalar loss surrogate on residual hidden states, with both teacher losses and gradient supervision; differentiate that scalar for updates. Compare to a value-only critic and a direct vector-gradient predictor. This is distinct from merely adding more states to the previous free-vector predictor.
- [Ilyas et al., Datamodels, 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.00622): model how a training subset changes an evaluated prediction. **Evaluation implication:** test candidate-subset rankings and the performance of the selected subset, using new starts and separate selection/evaluation populations. Training many teachers can erase first-use savings, so their cost is explicit.
- [Park et al., TRAK, ICML 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.14186): scalable attribution is evaluated through its ability to predict outcomes under changed training subsets. Our rollout surrogate is different from TRAK. Rank correlation alone is insufficient with only five nearly tied candidates; also report Oracle spread, selection regret, individual starts and uncertainty.
- [Xia et al., LESS, ICML 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.04333v3): data selection uses optimizer-aware gradient features and accounts for sequence-level normalization. **Implication:** an accurately predicted raw gradient need not imply an accurately predicted Adam training outcome. Compare actual rollouts and cheap true-gradient controls; any first-order scoring extension must explicitly state its approximation and cost.

Initial bounded priorities: (1) frozen CV16 transfer and matched controls; (2) loss-critic/Sobolev surrogate with state coverage; (3) cheaper online residual surrogates or short-rollout ranking if diagnostics justify them. A literature idea is not counted as attempted until its code is executed.

## Additional hypotheses registered before their rollouts

- **Explicit current-state input (our hypothesis):** H alone contains the current LoRA contribution mixed with a much larger frozen residual. Supply deltaY = scale * X A^T B^T explicitly, including its magnitude. This is invariant to equivalent rescaling of the LoRA factors and may help distinguish training stages. It is an architectural hypothesis, not a demonstrated benefit.
- **Compact matrix output (our hypothesis):** predict a low-dimensional approximation to C = G^T X, reconstructing current LoRA gradients analytically. This reduces output size but can lose token structure; projection-only errors are measured separately.
- [SAGA, Defazio et al., 2014](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2014/hash/937964195d6fb3a55cd7cc578165f058-Abstract.html) stores previous component gradients for incremental variance reduction. [SVRG, Johnson and Zhang, 2013](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2013/hash/ac1dd209cbcc5e5d1c6e28598e8cbbe8-Abstract.html) motivates periodic reference gradients. Our gradient-table control uses stale per-example suffix derivatives or factor gradients within a 32-example batch, with delayed corrections and optional coefficient adaptation. It then applies clipping and Adam; the original convex convergence claims do not transfer automatically. Zero initialization and fully charged bootstrap initialization are separate arms.
- [TinyBERT, Jiao et al., 2020](https://aclanthology.org/2020.findings-emnlp.372/) motivates using output distributions and intermediate representations as distillation targets. Our causal token critic and separate behavior readout test this idea in a different setting: a fixed Qwen suffix under varying LoRA states. Restricted ABC/answer-token probabilities are approximations, not full generative behavior.
- [Predicting Neural Network Accuracy from Weights, Unterthiner et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.11448) studies predicting model performance from weights on a large collection of small networks. It supports treating capability prediction as its own empirical task; it does not establish transfer for LoRA LLMs. Our behavior surrogate uses current adapter effects and input features, and must beat a state-insensitive baseline on unseen starts and candidates.

Readout evaluation uses a common fixed generated-answer template, so gold class identity is never fed into the behavior surrogate. Forced-format teacher classification and actual generated-answer accuracy are reported separately. A cheap exact teacher forced-format readout is included as an efficiency baseline.

## Local suffix linearization (registered before pilot outcomes)

[Fast Adaptation with Linearized Neural Networks, Maddox et al., 2021](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v130/maddox21a.html) studies Jacobian-based local representations around trained networks. [Task Arithmetic in the Tangent Space, Ortiz-Jimenez et al., 2023](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/d28077e5ff52034cd35b4aa15320caea-Abstract-Conference.html) studies explicit tangent-space fine-tuning for model editing. These motivate a local approximation; their empirical results do not imply it will work for this LoRA setup.

Our proposed suffix surrogate caches two teacher derivatives for the ABC logit differences relative to C, at a declared adapter anchor. For a new adapter, approximate logits by their anchor values plus the derivative dotted with the change in the layer-8 LoRA output. The current B A product is retained exactly. Softmax cross entropy on these approximate logits supplies a cheap predicted gradient. This replaces the frozen suffix computation with a local differentiable model and requires no neural-predictor fitting.

All Jacobian preparation and refresh costs are charged. Compare pure surrogate updates, periodic re-anchoring, corrections using the original full-answer loss, and exact teacher updates using the same restricted ABC objective. Restricted-class pure updates change the training objective; improvements cannot be attributed to surrogate fidelity without the matched class-objective control. Initial gradient algebra and teacher-gradient agreement are checked before rollouts. Generalization to new starts and candidate subsets remains untested at registration time.

[Linearization Explains Fine-Tuning in Large Language Models, Afzal et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.08239v1) explicitly studies proximity regularization and local linearity in LoRA. The paper's squared-loss/kernel assumptions do not directly cover our Adam/classification setup. It motivates a later development ablation with decoupled attraction to the starting LoRA factors (matched in exact-teacher controls), if fixed-anchor drift limits the initial tangent pilot. This option is implemented but not yet run or claimed beneficial.

## Common implementation controls

[PyTorch CUDA graph documentation](https://docs.pytorch.org/docs/stable/notes/cuda) describes reducing dispatch overhead for static shapes/control flow. A separate optional teacher-gradient probe is queued: capture the same padded cached-suffix computation, verify gradients for changed inputs and changed LoRA parameters, and measure capture cost plus replay with input packing. This is a potential optimization for **both Oracle and predictor corrections**. It is not counted as a predictor advantage, and no final recipe uses it without end-to-end validation. Padding never removes real input tokens. The installed environment is PyTorch2.13.0+cu130 and Transformers5.15.1; the documentation supplies general guidance, while actual local execution and numerical audits provide the compatibility check.

### Rollout-induced distribution shift (prospective, not yet implemented)

Ross, Gordon and Bagnell, *A Reduction of Imitation Learning and Structured Prediction to No-Regret Online Learning* (AISTATS 2011): https://proceedings.mlr.press/v15/ross11a.html . The paper studies sequential prediction where previous predictions change future inputs, and proposes iterative data aggregation. Our hypothesis: gradient predictors trained only at Oracle states may be evaluated at different CV-induced LoRA states; labeling development CV states and refitting could improve long rollouts. This is a DAgger-inspired adaptation, not a transfer of its theoretical guarantee to clipped Adam/LoRA. Compare with extra Oracle-state labels and charge all label/fit/trajectory costs. No heldout-start or selection outcomes may enter fitting.

### Teacher-width depth compression (implemented, queued)

Inspired by TinyBERT's teacher/student distillation (https://aclanthology.org/2020.findings-emnlp.372/), test a causal suffix retaining the original1536 hidden dimension, the layer8 MLP, and copied teacher decoder blocks18 and27. Fixed answer-token readout weights initialize from the teacher. Compare logit distillation alone with logit plus activation-gradient supervision. This architecture and block selection are our experiment, not a claim from TinyBERT. The input contains current hidden states and a causal mask, not the gold class; labels enter cross entropy only. All source labels remain predictor_train/dev. Actual fitting, collection, rollout and predictor-inference costs must be charged.

### Full-data memory correction (implemented, queued)

A second SAGA-inspired experiment maintains the predictor mean over the entire candidate dataset, rather than only the current32-example minibatch. IID population draws make the raw correction unbiased for the full-data token-mean objective before numerical rounding, clipping and Adam. Compare transported activation-gradient memory and flat factor-gradient memory, with paid full-dataset bootstrap and matching IID Oracle/CV controls. This differs from the earlier minibatch-table experiment; no convex SAGA convergence guarantee is claimed for LoRA/Adam.

### Bounded model use and surrogate exploitation: subsequent interpretation

These papers were consulted after the early-cutoff development experiment; they do not retroactively preregister or explain its numerical outcome.

[Janner et al., When to Trust Your Model: Model-Based Policy Optimization, NeurIPS 2019](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/2019/hash/5faf461eff3099671ad63c6f3f094f7f-Abstract.html) studies the tradeoff between inexpensive model-generated data and model bias, and uses short simulated rollouts starting from real data. The relevant analogy is to limit reliance on a learned training-dynamics model when its generalization weakens. Our early-CV cutoff is a diagnostic, not an implementation of MBPO, and its RL guarantees do not apply to clipped Adam/LoRA.

[Trabucco et al., Conservative Objective Models for Effective Offline Model-Based Optimization, ICML 2021](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/trabucco21a.html) addresses optimization exploiting overly optimistic learned scores away from the observed data distribution. This motivates a future loss-critic audit: compare the surrogate's predicted loss with the actual teacher loss along a pure-surrogate trajectory, then test conservative or constrained updates if optimism is observed. Current accuracy collapse and gradient drift alone do not prove that specific mechanism. COMs and a conservative loss objective have not been implemented in this study.

### Execution status at final recipe selection

The graph backend, copied teacher suffix, full-data memory correction, state-coverage fit, mean-aware fit, sparse calibration and anchored behavior readout have completed their declared development screens. Their outcomes are recorded in DEVELOPMENT.md and the experiment inventory; queued wording above preserves their original prospective motivation. The graph backend passed full512-step exact-weight and FLOP accounting audits. DAgger-style on-policy refitting and COMs-style conservative optimization were not executed. No paper's theoretical guarantee is claimed for this study.
